"Follow your passion" sounds inspiring until you try to do it. What if you do not know what your passion is? What if it does not pay the rent? What if you are good at something you do not love?

You do not need a lightning-bolt revelation. You need a process — and permission to start small.

## Start with energy, not job titles

Forget the question "What is my passion?" for a moment. Ask a better one: **what gives you energy, and what drains it?**

For one week, keep a simple note. At the end of each day, write down one thing that gave you energy and one thing that drained you. Do not overthink it. After seven days, a pattern usually appears — and it is far more honest than any career quiz.

Maybe you light up when you explain things to others. Maybe you drain fast in back-to-back meetings but come alive when you build something alone. That is data. Use it.

## The three-circle test

A direction worth pursuing usually sits where three things overlap:

- **What you are good at** — skills people already come to you for.
- **What you enjoy** — the work you would do even on a slow Tuesday.
- **What the world will pay for** — a real need, not just a hobby.

You do not need all three perfectly aligned today. Two out of three is enough to start. The third can be built.

## Test small before you leap

The biggest mistake is treating a career change as one giant decision: quit, or stay forever. There is a middle path — **small experiments**.

Interested in writing? Write one article a week for a month. Curious about teaching? Run a free one-hour session for friends. Drawn to design? Take one small freelance project. A month of experiments tells you more than a year of thinking about it.

And here is the important part: an experiment that fails is not a failure. It is information. You just learned what you do not want — which is half the answer.

## Make a 90-day plan, not a five-year plan

Five-year plans are guesses dressed up as certainty. A 90-day plan is something you can actually do:

- **Days 1–30:** energy audit + talk to three people doing work that interests you.
- **Days 31–60:** one small experiment, two hours a week.
- **Days 61–90:** decide — continue, adjust, or try the next experiment.

Ninety days from now, you will either have a direction or a much clearer sense of what is not for you. Both are progress.
